# Scored evaluation

Use this guide when a rating is requested. Choose criteria suited to the target, such as correctness, clarity, consistency, maintainability, usability, or completeness. Explain the criteria and any limits in the available evidence.

## Default scale

Use the user's scale when specified; otherwise use 0–10:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Fundamentally broken, missing, or unusable |
| 3–4 | Major gaps or weak foundations |
| 5–6 | Usable, with meaningful inconsistencies or omissions |
| 7–8 | Strong overall, with specific remaining gaps |
| 9 | Very strong, with minor weaknesses |
| 10 | Meets the relevant quality bar with no material improvement identified |

Ground the score in observations. Avoid fake precision, inflated ratings, or treating every dimension as equally important. Do not assign an unsupported score when essential evidence is missing.

## Output

- Give the score and explain the evidence behind it.
- Identify the main limitations and the most useful concrete improvements.
- When a path to higher levels is requested, describe what blocks each remaining level and the actions needed to reach it. Make each step meaningful; do not invent work to manufacture a ladder to 10.
- At 10, omit improvement steps unless new evidence identifies a gap.
